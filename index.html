What we are demanding vs Platform policy

Mandatory gov't flood data integration PAGASA & NOAH API
Current: Platforms use blind algorithms that force riders into flooded corridors and cut wet-weather pay by 20%.
Demand: Mandatory API integration of PAGASA/NOAH flood maps to auto-reroute flooded corridors, trigger +35% to +50% hazard pay, and pause speed timers.
Unrealistic delivery speed timers Safety Timers
Current: Fixed 25-min timers regardless of flooded streets, leading to 1-star weather penalties.
Demand: Dynamic timer extensions and complete nullification of customer rating penalties during rain alerts.
Arbitrary account suspension Due Process
Current: Algorithmic deactivation without human explanation when riders decline flooded trips.
Demand: 72-hour formal notice, UDRMM representation, and DOLE Labor Advisory 17 right-to-refuse protection.
Safety gear & medical coverage Worker Health
Current: 100% equipment, poncho, and injury expenses borne entirely by the rider.
Demand: Free company-issued rain ponchos & emergency medical clinic fund in all 16 cities.

ACADEMIC FRAMEWORK

Battling Global Giants Locally (B.G.G.L.)

Battling Global Giants Locally (B.G.G.L.) is our student taskforce crew for university MSM Assignment 2. The name represents our focus on supporting local workers in their struggle against the power of global platform companies, and the acronym B.G.G.L. is formed directly from the initials of the four students comprising this taskforce: Beatrice, Gyeong, Georgia, and Louise.

Academic Framework & Fictional Identity: This website is an academic prototype created exclusively as a university assignment (MSM Assignment 2). United Delivery Riders of Metro Manila (UDRMM) is a fictional worker-led union designed as a case study communication campaign to explore how gig couriers could organize against multinational delivery platforms. All actions, memberships, and campaign initiatives presented on this site are part of this fictional educational simulation.

📊 DOLE-OSHC Study (2023)

A Philippine Department of Labor and Employment study of 130 Metro Manila riders revealed that 91% have no formal employment contracts, 68% work >8 hours daily, and 40% suffered road injuries on duty (Sta Ana et al., 2023).

🛵 Fairwork Philippines (2025)

Across ~85,000 riders (Foodpanda ~45k, Grab ~40k), 0 out of 9 platforms guaranteed local minimum wage after factoring in fuel, phone data, and motorcycle maintenance. Riders bear 100% of operational risks (Fairwork, 2025).
